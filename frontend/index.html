Research Methodology

How FakeLens evaluates authenticity, detects manipulation signatures, and performs forensic source attribution across disparate visual domains.

1. Multi-Dataset Foundations: DeepFakeFace, ArtiFact & CIFAKE

FakeLens implements domain-isolated forensic intelligence across three authoritative benchmarks (24 models total across 5 families):

  • DeepFakeFace (60,000 images): Focused on facial deepfakes. Contains high-resolution Wikipedia human portraits (wiki), text-to-image synthesis (text2img), localized facial inpainting (inpainting), and facial identity swaps (insight). (10 models: 5 binary, 5 multiclass).
  • ArtiFact (55,000 images): Focused on general scene, object, animal, and texture forgery. Contains natural photographs (REAL), generative adversarial networks (GAN), latent diffusion (DIFFUSION), content-aware inpainting (INPAINTING), and transformer generation (TRANSFORMER). (10 models: 5 binary, 5 multiclass).
  • CIFAKE (119,326 decontaminated images): CIFAR-10 real vs Stable Diffusion v1.4 synthetic imagery. Evaluates native 32×32 RGB image representation, normalized flattened 3,072-pixel representations, and 100-dimensional fitted PCA embeddings across 4 model families (Custom CNN, MLP, Random Forest, KNN; binary only).

2. Architectural Paradigms: Deep Learning vs Classical Forensics

We systematically contrast deep feature representation learning against handcrafted domain-specific forensic features:

  • EfficientNet-B0 (Champion): Utilizes compound coefficient scaling to extract hierarchical spatial edge, noise, and semantic features. Achieves top performance across DeepFakeFace (91.87%) and ArtiFact (86.60%). (Not evaluated on CIFAKE).
  • Custom CNN: Convolutional backbones tailored to domain scale (FakeLensSimpleCNN for 224×224 DFF/ArtiFact; 3-block 293K parameter CNN for CIFAKE achieving 97.18% validation accuracy).
  • Classical Forensic ML (RF, MLP, KNN): Evaluated over a comprehensive 1,798-dimensional feature vector for DFF/ArtiFact, and flattened 3,072-D normalized pixels with 100-D PCA projection for CIFAKE.

3. Two-Stage Conditional Attribution & Disagreement Resolution

To ensure optimal user transparency, inference follows a hierarchical workflow:

1. Dedicated Binary Screening: High-recall binary model separates authentic images from manipulations.
2. Conditional Attribution: If flagged as FAKE, the matching model family multiclass engine identifies the generative mechanism.
3. Disagreement Detection: If the independent multiclass model classifies a flagged fake as authentic baseline, FakeLens issues an explicit warning banner explaining the differing perspectives.

About FakeLens

An academic machine learning system developed for comprehensive image forgery detection and attribution.

Project Purpose

As generative AI technologies advance, synthetic face generation, inpainting, and deepfake face-swapping have become pervasive. Most forensic detectors output only a binary label (Real or Fake) without explaining what kind of manipulation occurred. FakeLens bridges this gap by combining dedicated authenticity screening with multi-class source attribution across three independent datasets: DeepFakeFace (facial portraits), ArtiFact (general natural images & multi-generator scenes), and CIFAKE (CIFAR-10 real vs Stable Diffusion v1.4 synthesis). Each dataset maintains its independent models without cross-contamination.

Academic Disclaimer & Limitations

FakeLens is a university research prototype. Model outputs represent probabilities based on training dataset distributions (DeepFakeFace, ArtiFact, and CIFAKE) and do not constitute legal or absolute proof of authenticity. Each dataset is trained and evaluated independently; models are not evaluated for cross-dataset zero-shot generalization. Prediction probabilities are diagnostic research indicators rather than definitive guarantees.
